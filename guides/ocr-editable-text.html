How to Convert a Scan into Editable Text with OCR

Updated November 2026 · about 6 minutes to read

A scan is a picture, not text. To convert a scan into editable text you need OCR — optical character recognition. Until that step, every letter is just pixels: you cannot select it, copy it, or search for it. This guide explains what OCR actually does, the workflow that produces the best results, the details that measurably improve accuracy, and how to run the whole thing in your browser without uploading your document anywhere.

Why the text in a scan cannot be selected

PDFs produced by cameras or scanners are usually image-only PDFs: one page equals one bitmap. You can see the words, but the file contains no character data at all. That means:

  • Dragging over the text selects nothing, and copying yields an empty clipboard.
  • Searching for a keyword in a PDF reader returns zero hits.
  • Changing a date or an amount requires rebuilding the layout from scratch.
  • Quoting a sentence means retyping it by hand.

OCR reads those pixels back into characters. It locates text lines and character shapes, compares them against a language model, and outputs plain text with line breaks and spacing. For printed material this step is remarkably reliable.

The workflow

  1. Start from a clean scan. Roughly seventy percent of recognition quality is decided by the input image. Avoid shadows and skew, line the corners up with the frame, and apply a black & white or greyscale filter to flatten the background before exporting.
  2. Pick the right language. Choose the combined Chinese and English model for Chinese documents, and English only for English contracts. A wrong language choice visibly hurts accuracy, especially on digits and punctuation.
  3. Recognise page by page. Running pages individually makes it obvious which sheet was blurry, instead of forcing you to redo the whole document.
  4. Proofread the fields that matter. Check numbers, amounts, dates, names and reference codes. Those are exactly where OCR slips, and exactly where a mistake is expensive.
  5. Export as a text file. Save TXT for clean plain text, or Markdown when you want paragraph structure preserved for later pasting into documents, notes or a repository.

Five details that improve accuracy

1. Fill the frame with the page

The larger the sheet in the photo, the more pixels each character gets and the steadier recognition becomes. Cropping away the desk and your fingers beats taking another high-resolution shot of the same bad framing.

2. Add contrast, remove shadows

Shadows smear strokes into grey blobs. A black & white filter pushes the paper to pure white and the ink to pure black, which is the friendliest preprocessing for printed text.

3. Keep the page upright

Pages tilted by more than ten degrees confuse line segmentation. Correct perspective first so text lines run horizontally, then recognise.

4. Handle columns separately

Newspapers and magazines with multiple columns often get their lines stitched together across the gutter. Crop each column and recognise them one at a time, or reorder the text afterwards.

5. Lower your expectations for tables and handwriting

Cell borders, cursive handwriting and decorative headlines all reduce accuracy noticeably. Treat those results as drafts and verify them by hand before using them.

Frequently asked questions

Why can I not select text in a scanned PDF?

Most scanned PDFs are image-only: each page is a bitmap with no character data behind it. The text has to be recovered with OCR before it can be selected, copied or searched.

How accurate is OCR on printed documents?

On clean scans of printed text, accuracy is usually very high. Handwriting, decorative fonts, stamps and dense tables still require a manual proofread.

Does OCR upload my document?

With Aixuno Scan, recognition runs inside your browser. Once the language data is cached, it can work offline, and your scan is never sent to a server.

What formats can I export the recognised text to?

You can copy the text directly, or download it as a plain TXT file or a Markdown file that keeps paragraph structure.
