How to Scan Multiple Pages into One PDF

Updated November 2026 · about 6 minutes to read

Scanning multiple pages into one PDF is the standard need behind contracts, tender documents, medical records and textbooks. The process is straightforward: capture pages in a batch, confirm the crop and filter for each one, then export once and let the tool merge everything into a single multi-page PDF. What actually decides the quality is shooting order, consistency between pages, and file size control — each covered below.

First decide: one file or several?

Merging is not always better. The test is simple:

  • Merge when the pages form one continuous document — a contract, an agreement, a transcript, a multi-page form. Reviewers expect a single complete file.
  • Do not merge when the pages are unrelated records — invoices from different months, notes from different subjects. Separate, well-named files are easier to archive and search.
  • Compromise by grouping per purpose: front and back of an ID in one file, proof of income in another.

Also check the size limit of your submission channel. Email attachments commonly cap at 20–25 MB, and official portals are often stricter. Know the limit before you pick a resolution.

The workflow

  1. Order the paper first. Sort the originals into their final sequence before you start shooting. Fixing order afterwards means dragging pages around a list, and pages get missed.
  2. Fix the camera position. Prop the phone up or rest it on a stack of books so every shot covers the same area. A stable position means consistent page size and perspective once merged.
  3. Batch select or shoot in sequence. Aixuno Scan accepts several photos at once and also supports continuous capture with the camera. Batch imports follow filename order, so prefix filenames with 01, 02 and so on.
  4. Confirm the crop on every page. Auto-detection outlines each page; drag the corners on any page that drifted. Skipping this is what makes a document look amateur — one crooked page ruins the set.
  5. Keep filters and ratio consistent. Use one filter for the whole file. Choose A4 across the board if you will print it, or Auto for a purely digital archive.
  6. Count the pages, then export. Verify nothing is duplicated or missing, then export the PDF in one go.

Four details that make it read as one document

1. Keep the lighting identical

Shoot the whole batch in the same place under the same light so white point and colour temperature match. Moving to another desk halfway through is how you end up with five white pages and five yellow ones.

2. Leave the same margin on every page

Do not crop one page tight and leave a wide background on the next. A small, uniform margin keeps pages from jumping in size while reading.

3. Handle landscape pages deliberately

Inserted landscape tables look tiny in a portrait PDF and read badly. Rotate those pages during scanning, or put them in a separate file.

4. Watch page count and file size

Text pages run a few hundred kilobytes each with a black & white filter, so thirty pages still land in single-digit megabytes. If a single page exceeds about a megabyte, you are probably exporting the original photo at full resolution — switch the filter and the size drops sharply.

Frequently asked questions

Can a phone scan a dozen pages into a single PDF?

Yes. Select several photos at once or capture pages in sequence, confirm the crop and filter for each page, and export once. The pages merge into a single PDF in the order you added them.

What if the pages are in the wrong order?

Reorder the pages before exporting, or re-shoot the missing page and insert it. Photographing top to bottom and front to back reduces rework.

How do I make a multi-page PDF smaller?

Applying black and white or greyscale to text-only pages usually shrinks the file noticeably, and lower capture resolution helps too. A few hundred kilobytes per text page is normal.

Should every page use the same filter?

Yes, for consistency. Matching brightness, white point and colour across pages makes the document look official. If one page is much darker, re-shoot it rather than switching only that page to a different filter.
