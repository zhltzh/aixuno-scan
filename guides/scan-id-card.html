How to Scan an ID Card with Your Phone

Updated November 2026 · about 5 minutes to read

Scanning an ID card, passport or driving licence with a phone is easy to get wrong. What gets a submission rejected is rarely the camera — it is glare on the laminate, a cropped corner, or front and back delivered as two separate files. This guide covers the workflow, the five rules that make scans pass first time, and how to keep the image on your own device while you process it.

Why ID cards are harder than paper

Identity documents share a set of traits that work against photography:

  • Laminated surfaces create mirror reflections that land exactly on the name or the number.
  • Small, dense print means a slight focus miss is enough to make it unreadable.
  • Patterned backgrounds get destroyed by aggressive filters, taking the text with them.
  • Two mandatory sides, and the wrong order triggers a resubmission.
  • Sensitive data, where a leak is far more costly than a bad scan.

The workflow

  1. Place the card on a plain surface. A dark desk, a sheet of white paper or a dark card works — the point is clear contrast between the card edge and the background so edge detection succeeds on the first try.
  2. Turn the flash off and use diffused light. Daylight near a window, or a lamp aimed from the side and slightly above, moves the hotspot out of the critical area. Change your angle while watching the screen until the glare no longer covers the details.
  3. Shoot straight with margin around the card. Keep the phone parallel to the card and leave a border of background. Do not pinch the card with your fingers — they enter the frame and block light.
  4. Scan the portrait side first, then the reverse. Correct the crop on both. Use an enhancement filter rather than black & white so the security pattern and any stamps keep their colour and the file does not look altered.
  5. Merge into one PDF and name it clearly. Most portals want a single file. Include purpose and date in the filename, for example ID-front-back-insurance-2026-11.pdf.

Five rules that make it pass first time

1. Glare beats sharpness

A perfectly sharp photo with a hotspot over the number is still invalid. Accept slightly lower brightness in exchange for a completely glare-free number area.

2. Do not over-crop

All four corners must remain visible, and a little background is fine. Some reviewers check that the card outline is complete; a tight crop actually looks suspicious.

3. Keep the original aspect ratio

Export with Auto rather than forcing A4. ID cards have a fixed ratio, and stretching distorts the face and the text — an obvious sign of tampering.

4. Moderate resolution

Card text is small, so capture at a higher resolution, but do not export a huge file. Readable text in a few hundred kilobytes to about a megabyte is the sweet spot.

5. Process locally

An ID photo should never pass through a server you do not trust. Choose a tool that crops, filters and assembles the PDF inside the browser, with no upload during processing.

Sharing the result safely

After export, the file is yours and the remaining risk sits in how you share it. Submit only when genuinely required, annotate purpose and date in a blank area, avoid leaving ID photos in chat histories or public cloud folders, and delete them once the recipient no longer needs them. These habits protect you far more than any tool setting.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to scan both sides of an ID card?

Most online processes expect front and back in a single file. Scan the portrait side first, then the reverse, and export both pages into one PDF with the portrait side leading.

How do I avoid glare when scanning an ID?

Turn off the flash, light the card from the side or slightly above, and change the shooting angle slightly so the hotspot moves away from the printed details. Diffused daylight near a window works well.

Is my ID photo uploaded anywhere?

Aixuno Scan processes everything locally in your browser, so the image is not uploaded. The exported file still lives on your device, so manage storage and sharing yourself.

Should I watermark an ID scan?

For external submissions, annotate the purpose and date in a blank area, and consider adding a tiled watermark with a separate tool so the image cannot be reused for something else.
